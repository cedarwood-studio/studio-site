About

One senior engineer, and built to stay that way.

Cedarwood Studio is the practice of Jack Senechal, an infrastructure and platform engineer in Novato, California. He has been building and operating software systems since 2001, and spent the last decade of that inside the delivery problem specifically: the pipelines, the platforms, and the gates that decide whether a change is allowed to reach production.

Stance

What the studio believes

A delivery claim is only worth what you can re-run yourself. That is the whole stance, and most of what the studio does follows from it: the estimate comes from a dated spike rather than a feeling, the scope and the price are fixed before work starts, and the handoff includes the gates and the document that explains them, so that keeping the system does not require keeping us.

The studio is small deliberately. There is no bench to keep busy, which means there is no reason to recommend a larger engagement than the problem deserves — a one-day fix gets a one-day harness, not a fortnight of ceremony. And it never holds a client’s production credentials. Repository access is enough to do this work, and declining the rest removes a whole class of risk for both sides.

What the studio will not do is sell a standard package. The pipeline is written for your repository, your languages, and your test runner, then cross-checked against an internal list whose categories are published on the front page. The list keeps changing; the categories are the commitment.

How delivery is verified

Automation does the checking. A person does the judging.

We use modern automation and AI tooling in our delivery; every deliverable is reviewed by a human engineer before it reaches you.

What that buys you in practice is the thing the front page describes: the gates run on every change, so the review a person gives is of a system that has already been proven to build, test, scan, and deploy — not of a claim that it does.
